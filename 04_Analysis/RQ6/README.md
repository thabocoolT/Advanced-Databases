# RQ6 Analysis – Driver Net Earnings Per Hour After Estimated Fuel Costs

## Research Question

After estimating fuel costs, does average driver net earnings per hour meet the South African national minimum-wage benchmark of R30.23 per hour?

## Purpose

The purpose of this analysis is to determine whether drivers earn more than the R30.23 per hour benchmark after estimated fuel costs are deducted from their driver payouts.

## Method

Only completed trips were included in the analysis. Cancelled trips were excluded.

Driver earnings were calculated using the driver payout recorded in the dataset. Estimated fuel costs were calculated using:

**Estimated Fuel Cost = Distance × Vehicle Fuel Consumption ÷ 100 × Fuel Price**

Estimated net earnings were then calculated as:

**Estimated Net Earnings = Driver Payout − Estimated Fuel Cost**

Estimated net earnings per hour were calculated using:

**Estimated Net Earnings Per Hour = Total Estimated Net Earnings ÷ Total Trip Duration in Hours**

Petrol 95 prices were matched to the month of each trip. Inland fuel prices were used for Johannesburg, Pretoria, Vanderbijlpark and Potchefstroom, while coastal prices were used for Cape Town, Durban and Gqeberha.

The vehicle fuel-consumption values are model-level assumptions used to estimate fuel usage because the dataset does not contain actual fuel consumption for each trip.

## Overall Results

The analysis included 33,331 completed trips.

| Measure                         |  Result |
| ------------------------------- | ------: |
| Completed trips                 |  33,331 |
| Average driver payout           | R363.53 |
| Average estimated fuel cost     |  R31.27 |
| Average estimated net earnings  | R332.26 |
| Estimated net earnings per hour | R262.52 |
| Minimum-wage benchmark          |  R30.23 |

The estimated average driver net earnings were **R262.52 per hour**, which is above the R30.23 benchmark.

## Analysis

Cape Town recorded the highest estimated net earnings per hour at **R333.27**, followed by Johannesburg at **R295.62** and Durban at **R278.29**.

Potchefstroom recorded the lowest estimated net earnings per hour at **R218.09**. However, this is still well above the R30.23 benchmark.

The estimated fuel costs were relatively similar across the cities. Therefore, the differences in estimated net earnings per hour are mainly related to differences in driver payouts and trip duration rather than large differences in estimated fuel costs.

Overall, the results show that all seven cities have estimated driver net earnings per hour above the selected minimum-wage benchmark.

## Conclusion

The answer to RQ6 is **yes**. Based on the completed trips in the dataset and the estimated fuel costs, the average driver net earnings are above the R30.23 per hour benchmark.

The overall estimated net earnings per hour are **R262.52**, which is substantially higher than the R30.23 benchmark.

Cape Town has the highest estimated net earnings per hour at R333.27, while Potchefstroom has the lowest at R218.09.

## Limitations

The results should be interpreted as estimated net earnings and not actual driver profit.

The dataset does not contain actual fuel consumption for each vehicle or trip, so model-level fuel-consumption assumptions were used.

Other driver costs such as vehicle maintenance, insurance, depreciation, tyres, financing and other operating expenses were not included.

Therefore, the analysis only estimates earnings after fuel costs and does not represent the driver's complete real-world profit.
